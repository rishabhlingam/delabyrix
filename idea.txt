The proposed project will design and implement a multilingual, compliance-aware clinical AI agent and BI platform that bridges medical communication, cross-border health regulations, and predictive health intelligence. Built with human-in-the-loop oversight and data minimization, it dynamically updates across global registries (FDA, EU Union Register) to track legal statuses, medication availability, and privacy laws. The anticipated platform will be used by three types of end-users: 1) foreigners and international visitors who want to understand the local care system and navigate it, speak their language, explain complex consent and billing forms in simple terms, and address medication bans and restrictions; 2) healthcare providers and staff who work with foreign patients and need to explain some of the procedures to them in an adequate, culturally sensitive manner and follow local policies; and 3) care coordinators and administration who will be using the tool to monitor the care delivery process in different languages and respond to emerging needs by analyzing business analytic dashboards. Traditional translation tools are often not sufficient in diverse care settings as they typically interpret the meaning of each particular word and phrase but are unable to navigate the users through the process. It will facilitate consent and forms explanation, explain the differences between such similar concepts as intensive care and emergency room admission, help understand deductibles and copayments, and ensure compliance with local regulations. It will also be able to recognize banned, restricted, and delisted medications in different jurisdictions and maintain confidentiality and privacy throughout the interaction. Additionally, the tool will be able to analyze historic health, medication, and dietary data to identify patterns and recommend providers and services which will proactively manage care and reduce the overall burden on the population.


Value

For international travelers, foreign residents, and the clinicians and administrators who serve them, who struggle to understand and navigate an unfamiliar healthcare system, our agent delivers clear, culturally aware guidance in the user’s own language. It goes beyond word-for-word translation to explain consent and billing forms in plain terms, clarify the difference between services such as intensive care and the emergency room, and make sense of deductibles and copayments. It also flags medications that are banned, restricted, or delisted in a given country, and it protects privacy throughout. Providers get culturally sensitive communication support and local policy prompts, while care coordinators get dashboards that show how multilingual care is performing and where needs are emerging.

The proposed clinical AI translation system will address this challenge by providing the ability to understand and advise on a broad range of medical concepts.
A rules-based script or manual process cannot handle this problem well. The questions are open-ended, vary by language, culture, and jurisdiction, and depend on rules that change constantly. A script cannot interpret a confusing consent form, adapt its explanation to a patient’s background, or reason about why a billing term matters to them. Manual interpretation is slow, costly, and unavailable around the clock.
Agentic AI is needed because the task is a multi-step workflow rather than a single answer. The agent translates, interprets the medical meaning, adds cultural context, and then guides the user through next steps. It also monitors medication registries and privacy laws, updates its knowledge as they change, and analyzes historic health, medication, and dietary data to spot patterns and recommend services proactively. Human-in-the-loop oversight keeps high-stakes outputs safe and accountable.

It will help us design the pipeline architecture, generate and review code for the agent and BI dashboards, write tests, document the system, and check privacy and compliance logic, so we can deliver a reliable prototype faster.
